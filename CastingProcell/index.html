The read we are after

The film opens on a failure nobody has noticed yet and closes on a promise. The voice has to carry that arc without ever raising its volume — a senior operator telling a peer something they should already have known. Certain, unhurried, a little dry. Not a narrator selling batteries.

Apparent age 40 to 55. American English, no regional colour. What we screened out is anything that performs the copy — this script does its own work and punishes a voice that tries to help it.

All six reads here are female. That was not a decision we took going in; it is where the strongest takes landed. If you want a male counterpoint in front of you before you commit, say the word and we run a short second pass.

What to listen for

  • The first line Somewhere in your building, a device has already failed. It has to land as a plain statement of fact. Any reader who leans on failed has turned it into a scare, and the rest of the film has nowhere left to go.
  • The three lists Fire panel, car, printer. Locks, safes, dispensers, faucets. Battery level, maintenance, network. Three stacked lists in ninety seconds will sing-song on a weaker read. Listen for a voice that varies the weight inside each list instead of metering it out.
  • The sign-off Give your devices a voice. It is a line that invites sentiment and does not survive it. The best takes land it flat and let the idea do the lifting.
